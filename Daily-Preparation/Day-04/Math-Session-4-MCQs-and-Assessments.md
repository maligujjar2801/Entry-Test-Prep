## Part A — Function fundamentals

### Q1. Which statement correctly defines a function \(f:A\to B\)?

- A. Every element of \(A\) has at least two outputs.
- B. Every element of \(A\) has exactly one output in \(B\).
- C. Every element of \(B\) must have exactly one input.
- D. Every element of \(A\) must have a different output.

**Correct answer: B.** A function assigns exactly one output to each input in its domain. Different inputs may share an output.

### Q2. Consider the relation:

$$
R=\{(1,2),(2,3),(1,4),(3,5)\}
$$

Why is this relation not a function?

- A. Two different inputs have the same output.
- B. One input has two different outputs.
- C. Its domain contains three elements.
- D. Its range contains three elements.

**Correct answer: B.** Input 1 is paired with both 2 and 4.

### Q3. Which test determines whether a graph represents an injective function?

- A. Vertical-line test.
- B. Horizontal-line test.
- C. Diagonal-line test.
- D. Origin test.

**Correct answer: B.** A horizontal line must intersect an injective function's graph at most once.

## Part B — Domain and range

### Q4. Find the domain of:

$$
f(x)=\frac{1}{x^2-9}
$$

- A. \(\mathbb R\)
- B. \(\mathbb R\setminus\{3\}\)
- C. \(\mathbb R\setminus\{-3,3\}\)
- D. \([-3,3]\)

**Correct answer: C.** The denominator is zero at \(x=-3\) and \(x=3\).

### Q5. Find the domain of:

$$
f(x)=\sqrt{2x-6}
$$

- A. \((-\infty,3]\)
- B. \([3,\infty)\)
- C. \((3,\infty)\)
- D. \(\mathbb R\)

**Correct answer: B.** Require \(2x-6\ge0\), so \(x\ge3\).

### Q6. Find the range of:

$$
f(x)=(x-4)^2+2
$$

- A. \(\mathbb R\)
- B. \([0,\infty)\)
- C. \([2,\infty)\)
- D. \((2,\infty)\)

**Correct answer: C.** The minimum output is 2 and is attained at \(x=4\).

### Q7. What is the range of:

$$
f(x)=\frac{x+1}{x-2}
$$

- A. \(\mathbb R\)
- B. \(\mathbb R\setminus\{1\}\)
- C. \(\mathbb R\setminus\{2\}\)
- D. \(\mathbb R\setminus\{-1\}\)

**Correct answer: B.** The output cannot equal 1.

### Q8. The function is defined by:

$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x+2,&x<0,\\
x^2,&x\ge0.
\end{cases}
$$

What is its range?

- A. \(\mathbb R\)
- B. \([0,\infty)\)
- C. \((-\infty,2)\cup[0,\infty)\)
- D. \((-\infty,2]\cup[0,\infty)\)

**Mathematical range:**

$$
(-\infty,2)\cup[0,\infty)=\mathbb R
$$

The selected answer C is mathematically correct, but option A is also correct because it states the same set in simplified form. **This item was defective: it had two equivalent correct options.** The user's selected C is accepted; this flaw does not change the recorded 22/25 result.

## Part C — Transformations

### Q9. If \(f(x)=x^2\), what transformation produces:

$$
g(x)=(x-5)^2
$$

- A. 5 units left.
- B. 5 units right.
- C. 5 units up.
- D. 5 units down.

**Correct answer: B.** The vertex moves from \((0,0)\) to \((5,0)\).

### Q10. If \(g(x)=-f(x)\), the graph of \(f\) is:

- A. Reflected across the y-axis.
- B. Reflected across the x-axis.
- C. Shifted upward.
- D. Shifted right.

**Correct answer: B.** The negative sign outside the function changes every output \(y\) to \(-y\).

## Part D — Injectivity

### Q11. Is the function \(f(x)=x^2\) injective on \(\mathbb R\)?

- A. Yes, because its output is always non-negative.
- B. Yes, because every input has one output.
- C. No, because \(f(2)=f(-2)\).
- D. No, because it is not a function.

**Correct answer: C.** Distinct inputs 2 and -2 produce the same output 4.

### Q12. On which domain is \(f(x)=x^2\) injective?

- A. \(\mathbb R\)
- B. \([-2,2]\)
- C. \([0,\infty)\)
- D. \((-\infty,\infty)\)

**Correct answer: C.** On the non-negative half-line, \(x^2\) is strictly increasing.

### Q13. Suppose:

$$
f(a)=f(b)\implies a=b
$$

What property has been established?

- A. Surjectivity.
- B. Injectivity.
- C. Continuity.
- D. Periodicity.

**Correct answer: B.** This is the defining implication for an injective function.

### Q14. If a function is strictly decreasing on its entire domain, it must be:

- A. Surjective.
- B. Bijective.
- C. Injective.
- D. Constant.

**Correct answer: C.** Strict decrease prevents distinct inputs from sharing an output.

## Part E — Surjectivity

### Q15. Consider:

$$
f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R,\qquad f(x)=x^2
$$

Why is this function not surjective?

- A. Some inputs have no outputs.
- B. Negative real numbers in the codomain have no preimages.
- C. Its domain is too large.
- D. It is not injective.

**Correct answer: B.** Real squares cannot be negative.

### Q16. Which function is surjective with the indicated domain and codomain?

- A. \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R,\ f(x)=e^x\)
- B. \(f:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty),\ f(x)=x^2\)
- C. \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R,\ f(x)=x^2+1\)
- D. \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R,\ f(x)=2^x\)

**Correct answer: B.** The range of \(x^2\) on \(\mathbb R\) is exactly \([0,\infty)\).

### Q17. To prove that \(f:A\to B\) is surjective, which approach is correct?

- A. Show that different inputs produce different outputs.
- B. Show that every \(y\in B\) has at least one preimage \(x\in A\).
- C. Show that the domain contains zero.
- D. Show that the function is increasing.

**Correct answer: B.** This is the quantified definition of surjectivity.

## Part F — Bijectivity

### Q18. A function is bijective if it is:

- A. Injective only.
- B. Surjective only.
- C. Both injective and surjective.
- D. Continuous and increasing.

**Correct answer: C.**

### Q19. Consider:

$$
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty),\qquad f(x)=x^2
$$

This function is:

- A. Injective but not surjective.
- B. Surjective but not injective.
- C. Bijective.
- D. Neither injective nor surjective.

**Correct answer: C.** On this domain it is strictly increasing and reaches every non-negative output.

### Q20. Consider:

$$
f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R,\qquad f(x)=e^x
$$

Which statement is correct?

- A. Bijective.
- B. Injective but not surjective.
- C. Surjective but not injective.
- D. Neither injective nor surjective.

**Correct answer: B.** It is strictly increasing, but its range is only \((0,\infty)\).

## Part G — Inverse functions

### Q21. Find the inverse of:

$$
f(x)=3x-7
$$

- A. \(\dfrac{x-7}{3}\)
- B. \(\dfrac{x+7}{3}\)
- C. \(3x+7\)
- D. \(\dfrac{3}{x+7}\)

**Correct answer: B.** Exchange variables and solve \(x=3y-7\) for \(y\).

### Q22. If \(f:A\to B\) is bijective, then the inverse function has:

- A. Domain \(A\), codomain \(B\).
- B. Domain \(B\), codomain \(A\).
- C. Domain \(A\), codomain \(A\).
- D. Domain \(B\), codomain \(B\).

**Correct answer: B.** The inverse reverses the mapping.

### Q23. Find the inverse of:

$$
f(x)=x^2,\qquad x\in[0,\infty)
$$

- A. \(-\sqrt{x}\)
- B. \(\pm\sqrt{x}\)
- C. \(\sqrt{x}\)
- D. \(x^{-2}\)

**Correct answer: C.** The non-negative domain requires the non-negative square-root branch.

### Q24. The graph of an inverse function is the reflection of the original function's graph across:

- A. The x-axis.
- B. The y-axis.
- C. The line \(y=x\).
- D. The line \(y=-x\).

**Correct answer: C.**

### Q25. Suppose:

$$
f(x)=\frac{2x+3}{x-4}
$$

Its inverse is:

- A. \(\dfrac{4x+3}{x-2}\)
- B. \(\dfrac{4x-3}{x+2}\)
- C. \(\dfrac{2x+3}{x+4}\)
- D. \(\dfrac{x-4}{2x+3}\)

**Correct answer: A.** Exchanging x and y and solving gives \((4x+3)/(x-2)\).

## 3. Mistakes and corrections

### Q1 — Function definition

**Submitted:** C  
**Correct:** B

The definition of a function is that every element of the domain has exactly one output in the codomain:

$$
\forall x\in A,\ \exists!y\in B\text{ such that }y=f(x)
$$

The mistake confuses a function with a stronger property. A function does not require every codomain element to have exactly one input. That is not part of the basic definition.

Remember:
- Each input has exactly one output.
- Different inputs may share an output.
- Injectivity is the extra condition that different inputs cannot share an output.

### Q9 — Horizontal translation

**Submitted:** C (5 units up)  
**Correct:** B (5 units right)

For:

$$
f(x)=x^2,\qquad g(x)=(x-5)^2
$$

the minimum occurs at $x=5$. The vertex shifts from $(0,0)$ to $(5,0)$.

Recognition rule:

$$
f(x-a)\Rightarrow\text{right by }a
$$

The change is inside the input, so this is horizontal rather than vertical.

### Q10 — Reflection across an axis

**Submitted:** C (shifted upward)  
**Correct:** B (reflected across x-axis)

For:

$$
g(x)=-f(x)
$$

the output changes from $y$ to $-y$. This reflects the graph across the x-axis.

Compare:

$$
f(-x)\Rightarrow\text{reflection across y-axis}
$$

$$
-f(x)\Rightarrow\text{reflection across x-axis}
$$

A negative sign inside the input changes the horizontal coordinate; a negative sign outside changes the vertical coordinate.

## 5. Transformations and scaling — highest revision priority

The final assessment showed errors on both transformation questions:

- Horizontal translation was confused with vertical translation.
- Reflection across the x-axis was confused with a vertical shift.

The user also explicitly identified graph scaling and transformations as difficult.

The taught rules were:

$$
f(x-a)\Rightarrow\text{right by }a
$$

$$
f(x+a)\Rightarrow\text{left by }a
$$

$$
f(x)+a\Rightarrow\text{up by }a
$$

$$
f(x)-a\Rightarrow\text{down by }a
$$

$$
f(-x)\Rightarrow\text{reflection across y-axis}
$$

$$
-f(x)\Rightarrow\text{reflection across x-axis}
$$

Horizontal and vertical scaling were introduced in the original comprehensive artifact, but were not fully reinforced or assessed in the later interactive teaching sequence. They should be handled in a separate focused lesson:

$$
g(x)=af(x),\qquad g(x)=f(ax)
$$

Do not classify scaling as mastered or completed until the user has studied and practised it.

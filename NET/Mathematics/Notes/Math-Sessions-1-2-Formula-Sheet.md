# Math Sessions 1–2 — Formula & Recognition Sheet

## Straight Lines

### Distance

$$
d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}
$$

### Slope

$$
m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
$$

### Slope-intercept form

$$
y=mx+c
$$

### Point-slope form

$$
y-y_1=m(x-x_1)
$$

### Two-point form

$$
y-y_1=
\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}(x-x_1)
$$

### General form

$$
Ax+By+C=0
$$

$$
m=-\frac AB
$$

### Parallel lines

$$
m_1=m_2
$$

### Perpendicular lines

$$
m_1m_2=-1
$$

### Horizontal / vertical

$$
y=k\quad\Rightarrow\quad m=0
$$

$$
x=k\quad\Rightarrow\quad m\text{ is undefined}
$$

### Intercept form

$$
\frac xa+\frac yb=1
$$

# Circle

$$
(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2
$$

Centre:

$$
(h,k)
$$

Radius:

$$
r
$$

Expanded form:

$$
x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0
$$

Centre:

$$
\left(-\frac D2,-\frac E2\right)
$$

Radius:

$$
r=\sqrt{\frac{D^2+E^2}{4}-F}
$$

---

# Parabola

Horizontal:

$$
(y-k)^2=4a(x-h)
$$

Vertex:

$$
(h,k)
$$

Focus:

$$
(h+a,k)
$$

Directrix:

$$
x=h-a
$$

Vertical:

$$
(x-h)^2=4a(y-k)
$$

Focus:

$$
(h,k+a)
$$

Directrix:

$$
y=k-a
$$

Latus rectum:

$$
4|a|
$$

---

# Ellipse

Horizontal:

$$
\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}+
\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1
$$

with conventional notation

$$
a>b>0.
$$

Focal relation:

$$
c^2=a^2-b^2
$$

Eccentricity:

$$
e=\frac ca,\qquad 0<e<1
$$

Latus rectum:

$$
\frac{2b^2}{a}
$$

---

# Hyperbola

Horizontal:

$$
\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2}-
\frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1
$$

Focal relation:

$$
c^2=a^2+b^2
$$

Eccentricity:

$$
e=\frac ca>1
$$

Asymptotes:

$$
y-k=\pm\frac ba(x-h)
$$

Latus rectum:

$$
\frac{2b^2}{a}
$$

---

# Fast Recognition

| Equation pattern | Identify |
|---|---|
| \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\) | Circle |
| One variable squared | Parabola |
| Two positive squared terms | Circle/Ellipse |
| Opposite signs between squared terms | Hyperbola |
| \(c^2=a^2-b^2\) | Ellipse |
| \(c^2=a^2+b^2\) | Hyperbola |

## High-risk traps

- Centre signs reverse when reading from expanded form.
- Vertical line slope is undefined, not zero.
- Perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals.
- Ellipse: \(c^2=a^2-b^2\).
- Hyperbola: \(c^2=a^2+b^2\).
- The ellipse convention \(a>b\) should not be blindly imposed on a hyperbola.
- For a parabola, the squared variable indicates the direction of opening.
